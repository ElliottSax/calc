## Introduction
Dividend investing is a popular strategy for generating passive income and building long-term wealth. While many investors focus on high-priced dividend stocks, there are numerous quality dividend stocks under $50 that offer attractive yields and growth potential. In this article, we will look at well-known dividend payers that have traded under $50 a share, explain how to judge their yields, payout ratios, and growth rates, and discuss a strategy for incorporating them into your portfolio.

## Analysis
When evaluating dividend stocks under $50, it's essential to consider several key metrics. The current yield, which is the annual dividend payment divided by the stock price, is a critical factor in determining the attractiveness of a dividend stock. Additionally, the payout ratio, which is the percentage of earnings paid out as dividends, should be reasonable to ensure the sustainability of the dividend payments. Finally, the growth rate of the dividend payments is crucial in determining the potential for long-term wealth creation.

Well-known dividend payers whose shares have recently traded under $50 include:
- **AT&T Inc. ([T](/stocks/T))**: A telecom with a high current yield. AT&T cut its dividend in 2022 after spinning off WarnerMedia, so judge it on today's payout coverage, not its pre-2022 history.
- **Verizon Communications ([VZ](/stocks/VZ))**: The other large U.S. telecom, with a high yield and a multi-year streak of small annual increases.
- **Ford Motor Company ([F](/stocks/F))**: A low-priced, cyclical automaker whose dividend was suspended in 2020 and restored in 2021 — a reminder that cyclical dividends can disappear in a downturn.
- **Pfizer ([PFE](/stocks/PFE))**: A large pharmaceutical company that has paid a dividend for decades and has traded in the $20s and $30s since its post-pandemic decline.
- **Kinder Morgan ([KMI](/stocks/KMI))**: A natural-gas pipeline operator with a high yield backed largely by fee-based contracts.

## Comparison
When comparing dividend stocks under $50, it's essential to consider their industry, competitive advantage, and growth prospects. AT&T and Verizon are telecoms with slow growth but large, steady cash flows. Ford operates in the cyclical automotive sector, where earnings — and dividends — swing with the economy. Pfizer's dividend depends on a drug pipeline replacing revenue from products losing patent protection. Kinder Morgan's distributions rest on long-term pipeline contracts but remain sensitive to energy prices and interest rates.

Valuation and payout ratios differ widely across this group, and the highest yield is rarely the safest one. A yield that is far above a company's peers usually signals that the market doubts the dividend will be maintained. Compare each company's payout ratio with its own history and its sector before assuming a high yield is a bargain.

## Strategy
To incorporate these dividend stocks under $50 into your portfolio, consider the following strategy:
1. **Diversification**: Spread your investments across multiple sectors and industries to minimize risk.
2. **Dividend yield**: Target a dividend yield that is higher than the overall market average to generate attractive income.
3. **Payout ratio**: Ensure the payout ratio is reasonable to ensure the sustainability of the dividend payments.
4. **Growth rate**: Consider the growth rate of the dividend payments to determine the potential for long-term wealth creation.
5. **Valuation**: Evaluate the P/E ratio and other valuation metrics to determine if the stock is trading at a reasonable price.

By following this strategy and considering the analysis and comparison of these dividend stocks under $50, you can create a diversified portfolio that generates attractive income and has the potential for long-term growth.
