## Introduction
Master Limited Partnerships (MLPs) have long been a staple of income-focused investment portfolios, offering a unique combination of high dividend yields and tax efficiency. As a dividend investor, understanding the ins and outs of MLPs is crucial to unlocking their full potential. In this guide, we'll delve into the world of MLPs, exploring their benefits, risks, and key metrics to consider when building a dividend portfolio. We'll also name several of the largest MLPs still trading today and explain how to read their yields, payout ratios, and growth rates for yourself.

## Analysis
MLPs are publicly traded partnerships that combine the benefits of corporate and partnership structures. They are typically involved in the energy sector, including oil and gas production, transportation, and storage. One of the primary advantages of MLPs is their tax efficiency, as they are pass-through entities that avoid double taxation. This means that MLPs are only taxed at the individual level, rather than at both the corporate and individual levels, resulting in higher dividend payouts for investors. Additionally, MLPs are required to distribute a significant portion of their cash flow to unitholders, making them an attractive option for income-seeking investors.

When evaluating MLPs, it's essential to consider key metrics such as current yield, payout ratio, and growth rate. The current yield represents the annual dividend payment per share, divided by the stock's current price. The payout ratio, on the other hand, measures the percentage of cash flow distributed to unitholders. A lower payout ratio indicates a more sustainable dividend payment, while a higher ratio may suggest a higher risk of dividend cuts. The growth rate, meanwhile, reflects the MLP's ability to increase its dividend payments over time.

## Comparison
The largest MLPs still trading on U.S. exchanges include Enterprise Products Partners ([EPD](/stocks/EPD)), MPLX LP ([MPLX](/stocks/MPLX)), Plains All American Pipeline ([PAA](/stocks/PAA)), Energy Transfer ([ET](/stocks/ET)) and Western Midstream Partners ([WES](/stocks/WES)). All five are midstream (pipeline and storage) businesses, and all pay distributions that have typically yielded well above the S&P 500's dividend yield. We don't quote their current yields or payout ratios here because those figures change every trading day — click a ticker for live data, and compare distributable cash flow (not GAAP earnings) against the distribution when you judge payout coverage.

A caution that matters for MLP investors: the sector has consolidated. Several once-popular partnerships no longer exist as separate securities — Valero Energy Partners was absorbed by Valero in 2019, Phillips 66 Partners by Phillips 66 in 2022, and Magellan Midstream Partners by ONEOK in 2023. Older articles (and some screeners) still list them, so verify that a partnership still trades before you plan around its yield.

## Strategy
When building a dividend portfolio with MLPs, it's essential to consider a long-term approach. MLPs can be volatile, and their dividend payments may fluctuate over time. However, by focusing on established MLPs with strong track records, investors can minimize risk and maximize returns. It's also crucial to diversify across different sectors and industries to reduce exposure to any one particular market or economic downturn.

In addition to selecting individual MLPs, investors may also consider MLP-focused exchange-traded funds (ETFs) or mutual funds. These funds offer a diversified portfolio of MLPs, providing broad exposure to the sector while minimizing individual stock risk. Two of the largest are the Alerian MLP ETF (AMLP) and the Global X MLP ETF (MLPA).

## Conclusion
Master Limited Partnerships offer a compelling investment opportunity for dividend-focused investors, providing a unique combination of high yields, tax efficiency, and growth potential. By understanding the key metrics and characteristics of MLPs, investors can make informed decisions and build a diversified portfolio that meets their income goals. Large, fee-based midstream partnerships like Enterprise Products Partners and MPLX have historically offered high distribution yields backed by long-term contracts, though no yield is guaranteed and the sector remains sensitive to energy prices and interest rates. As the energy landscape continues to evolve, MLPs are likely to remain a vital component of dividend portfolios, offering a reliable source of income and long-term growth potential.
